# Character and journal (W5)

An enabled world can opt into `journal`, the versioned `cyoa.world-journal` v1 extension. Creator's **Locations and time → Character and journal** and MCP's existing world read/review/Apply share the same service, immutable history, exact review, separate author consent and retry-safe operation receipts. This checkpoint adds presentation and authored discovery; it does not add a new simulation engine or generated narration.

## Canonical character values

Character rules select existing canonical Mechanics stats or relationships and an AND-list of visibility triggers. Unlisted values are hidden. An empty trigger list means always visible; a nonempty list is evaluated against the **current** committed state each time the view is read. Visibility can therefore disappear again when a stat, milestone, location or chapter condition changes. Each visible row uses the canonical label and current value. No independent character score, implicit relationship mapping, preference, decay or generated profile is introduced.

`read_world` returns bounded stat and relationship options and the mechanics version for authoring. A journal review binds that exact version. Publication projects canonical keys, categories, labels, initial values and bounds into `cyoa.native-journal` v1; compilation verifies the exact upstream projection and native bundle validation checks the canonical keys/categories/bounds/initials. Native mechanic records do not themselves include display labels. Historical builds use their exact original mechanics, rather than today's labels or scales.

The W5 Character view replaces the old unconditional Status panel for games that enable the extension; legacy `visibleMechanics` cannot disclose an omitted W5 value. Legacy abstract resources are not new W5 character attributes; typed inventory/currency remains in W4's dedicated views. Games without W5 retain their existing configuration and presentation.

## Triggers and observations

All conditions in a trigger list must hold; an empty list means always. W5 uses the shared chapter, milestone, current location, absolute game-minute, item-quantity and relationship predicates. It also supports inclusive canonical stat ranges and a named W2 event's minimum completed count. Event inspection/discovery alone never counts as completion. Item triggers require authored W4 items; relationship triggers in the journal use canonical state even without W4. Stat and relationship ranges require at least one bound. Time starts inclusively and ends exclusively. Unknown references, malformed bounds and contradictory ranges are rejected.

Journal discovery occurs only on **committed observations**: initialization, successful narrative choices after their effects/arrival, successful world actions after effects/arrival, and relevant intermediate time boundaries during waiting or stationary actions. Accepted W3 completion ordering is preserved: completion-time discovery follows all effects. Earlier valid observations remain remembered. Optional actions still cannot skip required encounters; failed actions roll back their provisional observations. Travel observes its destination on arrival, consistent with W3; it does not observe people or transient information along an unauthored journey.

Inspection, opening/closing views, map inspection, previews, cancellation and saving are free: they do not advance turns/time, observe new information, execute effects or write an autosave. There is no wall clock or provider involved. Authored narrative prose, choice feedback and artwork remain authored content; visibility rules do not automatically redact information an author deliberately puts into those surfaces.

## Places, entries, objectives and events

The current location and its containing areas become known on arrival. Authors can add location-reveal rules to disclose other places when triggers hold, including an empty list for initially known destinations. Known places remain remembered. In W5-enabled games, map area selectors, markers, destination details and travel buttons filter undiscovered locations. Direct travel to an undiscovered destination is rejected; an explicitly authored narrative/event/chapter arrival can discover its destination. Authors must reveal intended exits or provide authored arrivals; these controls do not prove exploration is solvable. Map artwork is a static authored image and cannot automatically redact labels baked into its pixels.

Journal entries contain a stable ID, title, authored text and reveal triggers. A satisfied entry is remembered once; later loss of its conditions does not erase its title/text. Entries display in authored order rather than claiming a detailed chronological log.

Objectives contain a stable ID, title, description, reveal triggers and completion triggers. Revealed objectives stay known. Completion is recorded once an objective is revealed and its completion conditions hold at an observation. A previously completed event can satisfy completion when an objective is later revealed; a transient completion condition missed before reveal is not retroactively reconstructed. Empty completion triggers complete an objective immediately when revealed. There is no automatic chapter expiry, failure or cancellation state for objectives; revealed objectives remain active until their completion triggers are observed. The dedicated view shows only revealed active objectives and titles of completed objectives, with no hidden counts or future instructions.

Recorded events reuse W2's existing **completed-event ledger**, showing the authored label/result, count and last action-start game time. Available or merely discovered encounters are not completed records. Repeats update one record rather than adding duplicate cards. No new narration is generated and no future schedule is exposed.

## Dedicated player views and keyboard access

W5 supplies one contextual navigation layer: Explore, Character, Journal, and W4's Inventory/Shops/Gifts when enabled. A secondary view hides exploration controls, illustrated scene, narrative prose/outcome and choices while retaining the current location, clock and game controls. Journal has dedicated Objectives, Entries, Places and Events sections. Views use native buttons with pressed state and normal Tab/Shift+Tab, Enter and Space behavior; selecting a view focuses its heading, and Back/Escape returns to Explore with focus on its navigation button. Game dialogs retain their existing cancellation/focus behavior. Viewing information remains possible while an encounter prevents game actions.

## Authoring, MCP, persistence and saves

Creator provides canonical visibility selectors, structured triggers, location reveals, journal prose and objective controls, plus a human-readable review. Edits clear review/consent. Dependency guards name journal references before an item, location, chapter, milestone or event can be deleted, and explain dependencies before disabling progression or inventory. Missing canonical visibility bindings remain visible with a warning and explicit removal controls, so authors can clean up removed stats/relationships while preserving other journal prose. Canonical reference changes require a fresh valid review; definitions are not silently retargeted.

New read-only `inspect_world_journal` validates a supplied state against the fresh current world and returns only currently visible character values, known places, revealed entries, active/completed objectives and completed-event records, plus world version and providerCalls 0. It uses existing project/loopback authentication and request/result bounds. It neither advances gameplay nor repairs an older state for a changed world. Authoring continues through shared `read_world`, `review_world`, separately authorized `apply_world`; inspection is not approval or publication.

The optional save extension `state.world.journal` v1 stores bounded, unique sorted ID arrays for known locations, entries and revealed/completed objectives. It deep-clones with history. Validation checks shape/version/references/bounds, completion membership, and that current observations have been recorded. Saves, autosave, manual load, rewind and restart restore their own discovery state; failed persistence retries save the already committed session without replaying actions. W2 event records remain in their original ledger. Full/privacy project archives, verified backups, duplication and immutable historical builds retain the definition through existing aggregate paths; no database migration or dependency was added.

The journal is absent from legacy worlds/saves and does not change their shapes or fingerprints. Saves remain bound to exact published bundles; there is no silent migration of old saves to new definitions. A recognized historical discovery flag is remembered state, not a cryptographic or exhaustive proof of the entire playthrough. Validation cannot reconstruct every past trigger from today's values. Offline source and authored rules necessarily remain inspectable; these views control ordinary presentation, not secrecy against a person inspecting or modifying a local package.

Standalone HTML and static ZIP embed the same native definition/runtime and work after Creator shuts down, with no API/provider calls. Previously downloaded packages require re-exporting to receive runtime changes. Markdown/Twee explicitly report unsupported world capabilities rather than dropping journal/visibility behavior. The existing 48,000-byte world definition and RPC/save budgets remain; collection maxima do not guarantee all maxima fit simultaneously. W6 demonstration work, broader UI/performance proof, crafting, equipment and combat are outside this checkpoint.
