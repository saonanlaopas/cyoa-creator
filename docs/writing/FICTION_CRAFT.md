# Fiction Craft Guide

Version: 0.1 — proposed shared defaults, 2026-11-03.

Purpose: support specific, coherent, lived-in fiction across genres and formats. Derived from the user's Story Craft Lab and Narrative Workshop discussion. This guide supplies craft defaults, not story canon, a mandatory genre, or permission to bypass content boundaries. Explicit task requirements and approved project style take precedence over these defaults; report unresolved canon conflicts rather than silently rewriting facts.

## Establish the assignment

Before drafting, read the relevant project brief, style direction, character records, preceding events, and scene or branch state. Establish viewpoint, tense, audience, content boundaries, format, length, and the scene's purpose. Preserve established choices; do not impose third person, past tense, adult themes, romance, or a literary register on every project. If information is missing, make modest compatible assumptions; ask when the uncertainty would materially change canon or the requested result. Do not claim to have read unavailable sources.

## People before payoff

Give the reader something specific to value before asking for grief, delight, fear, recognition, or romantic investment. Build ordinary habits, competence, irritations, obligations, private jokes, contradictions, and unfinished relationships. A person can be loved without becoming idealized. Do not substitute biography labels for behavior.

Each active participant should want something, however small. Let their wants shape what they say, avoid, misunderstand, notice, and do. Background figures need not all acquire miniature arcs. Characters may grow, contradict themselves, or behave badly, but changes should arise from circumstances and established capacities rather than author convenience.

## Dialogue with a lived scene around it

Do not default to long strings of one-line replies, symmetrical banter, or isolated emotional punchlines. Develop important conversations through the viewpoint character's attention and interpretation, competing intentions, physical circumstances, and changes in the relationship. A spoken line should belong to an unfolding encounter rather than float between interchangeable speakers.

Use action beats when they reveal intention, alter the situation, locate the speakers, or make a response legible. Avoid attaching a shrug, glance, smile, tightened jaw, or handled cup to every line. Allow a clean exchange when its pace is earned. Short dialogue is not a defect; unsupported dialogue as the dominant texture is the problem.

Make voices distinct through vocabulary, rhythm, directness, humor, restraint, silence, and what each person considers worth answering. People can interrupt, evade, misunderstand, answer sideways, or speak plainly. Do not make everyone equally witty or evasive. Read important exchanges aloud during revision.

## Paragraphs, rhythm, and interior life

Build coherent paragraphs around a connected action, perception, thought, or developing interaction. Use developed paragraphs where the scene needs room to accumulate meaning. Start a new paragraph when the speaker or focus changes; do not merge speakers merely to meet a length quota. Avoid habitual vertical fragmentation and one-line dramatic narration. Reserve isolation for emphasis that the surrounding scene has earned.

Vary sentence and paragraph length with the scene's pressure and intended reading surface. No minimum sentence count applies. Avoid both padding and uniformly clipped prose. Preserve clear dialogue attribution and the project's thought-formatting convention. Free indirect thought, direct thought, and plain emotional statements are legitimate tools; not every feeling must become a bodily symptom.

## Detail that belongs to these people

Choose details that affect action, understanding, memory, atmosphere, access, pleasure, risk, or choice. Ordinary objects and routines can carry continuity as their meaning changes. Let care include attention, practical help, curiosity, patience, and inconvenient effort, without requiring every relationship to follow that pattern.

Use sensory detail selectively; do not inventory all five senses on schedule. Source comparisons from the viewpoint's experience where appropriate. Remove decorative imagery that could be moved unchanged into an unrelated story. Quiet beauty, play, and rest are valid purposes too: every object need not become a symbol or a threat.

## World, continuity, and knowledge

Make unfamiliar systems understandable through what they permit, prevent, cost, or change. Use direct explanation when it improves clarity; do not require the reader to decode every fact from implication. Institutions may enter through daily obligations, but institutional conflict is optional, not a universal genre requirement.

Track time, location, physical positions, possessions, injuries, promises, discoveries, relationships, and unresolved consequences. Separate author knowledge from each character's knowledge. Private thoughts, hidden histories, future events, and offstage conversations are not automatically shared facts. Inference may be wrong and should be grounded in available evidence.

In action, maintain comprehensible cause and effect: position, movement, response, available time, effort, environment, and aftermath. Do not interrupt urgent moments with implausibly lengthy reflection. Let injuries and exhaustion matter afterward without turning every movement into choreography.

## Scene shape and earned endings

A scene should change something: circumstances, knowledge, obligation, intimacy, danger, expectation, or the reader's understanding. Quiet scenes can matter without a plot twist. Establish enough physical and social orientation to follow events, but avoid mechanically opening every scene with weather, waking, or a setting inventory.

Choose where to dramatize, summarize, or skip time. Expand meaningful changes; compress repetition and connective logistics. Scenes can connect through consequence, reversal, contrast, echo, parallel, or delay. Chronology alone is not automatically wrong, but inspect sequences that feel like unrelated episodes.

Earn confessions, forgiveness, rescue, victory, recognition, sacrifice, and other payoffs through prior attachment and pressure. Happiness need not be punished with a compulsory cost; tragedy need not be beautified. Avoid ending every scene with a quotable lesson, tidy emotional summary, or the same unresolved gesture. Let the ending fit what actually changed.

## When the fiction is interactive

Respect player-controlled decisions. Do not narrate consequential commitments, forgiveness, purchases, travel, or intimate consent as already chosen when they belong to the player. Distinguish page navigation from meaningful choices. Make intent understandable without revealing every future outcome.

Conditional prose must match actual knowledge and state, including refused invitations, missed events, and alternate routes. A described action is not an implemented mechanic. Use the project's canonical systems for promised state changes. Keep refusal and independent paths consistent with the project's intended experience; do not covertly punish players for declining romance.

## Revision and delivery

First check scene purpose, continuity, character knowledge, and branch logic. Then inspect wants, changes, transitions, and the balance of scene versus summary. Read dialogue for interchangeable voices, symmetrical quips, unsupported exchanges, and repetitive gestures. Finally revise repeated constructions, stock body reactions, redundant explanation, ornamental comparisons, and default perception filters such as “she noticed.” Keep a filter when noticing itself matters.

Evaluate patterns in context, not by automatic bans or numerical scores. Do not inflate word counts with recaps, repeated shared scenes, or filler. Keep fiction free of unsolicited process notes, offers to continue, and explanatory wrap-ups. Place necessary authoring reports outside the narrative. Deliver in the requested medium—chat, project records, files, or another supported format—and preserve applicable review and approval gates.
